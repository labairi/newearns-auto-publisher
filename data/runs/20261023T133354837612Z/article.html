Working out how to make money online mostly comes down to two things: choosing a platform, then living with its fees, thresholds and payout rules. This guide covers five methods in depth. Every fee and threshold comes from the platform's own help pages. It doesn't promise any income. It shows what each platform takes, what you need before your first payment, and the catch most lists skip. Freelancing on a marketplace

Freelancing suits you if you already have a skill clients pay for: writing, design, development, bookkeeping or marketing. Upwork is a useful example because its fee rules are clearly documented. If you later want to grow from solo freelancer to a team, our guide on starting a digital marketing agency covers that next step.

What Upwork takes

According to Upwork's Freelancer Service Fee page, the fee "ranges from 0% to 15% per contract". You see the percentage when a client sends you an offer or when you submit a proposal, and once the contract begins, "the fee is fixed and won't change." Upwork also states that it is not offering any regular discounts.

Upwork's own worked example: if you want to take home $20 per hour and your fee is 10%, charge the client $22.22 per hour. Over 12 hours, that gives net earnings of $239.98, which Upwork notes is $0.02 less than estimated because of rounding (source). To set your own rate, divide your target take-home rate by (1 minus your fee percentage). At a 10% fee, that's $20 ÷ 0.90.

Some contract types are treated differently, according to the same page:

  • Upwork Payroll and Any Hire contracts: no Freelancer Service Fee.
  • Direct Contracts: a reduced fee.
  • Enterprise clients: "typically" a 10% fee, with the exact rate depending on the contract terms.
  • Refunds: if your client is refunded, Upwork returns the service fee you paid on that amount.

The cost of applying for work: Connects

Connects are the virtual tokens you spend to submit proposals. Per Upwork's Connects help page, they cost $0.15 each and are sold in bundles. To buy them, you need a verified billing method on your account, "even if you plan to use your existing earnings to pay for them." The number of Connects a job needs varies, and it can change while the job is posted.

That makes job hunting a small, real expense. Before you spend Connects, Upwork suggests checking whether the client has already hired anyone, and reviewing the client's history and feedback from other freelancers (source). Upwork's menus can change, so the screen where you buy Connects may look different on your account.

Ways to get free Connects

All of these are conditional, according to Upwork:

  • Upwork runs offers "at times", and "some freelancers may receive 10 free Connects each month."
  • You earn 30 free Connects for each talent badge: Rising Talent, Top Rated and Top Rated Plus.
  • You "may" earn 12 free Connects when you submit three or more proposals and spend at least 54 Connects doing so.

Selling on Etsy

Etsy fits people who make handmade goods or digital products such as printables and templates. It charges several separate fees, so add them all up before you price anything. All figures below come from Etsy's Fees & Payments Policy (last updated October 5, 2026). Etsy lists its fees exclusive of VAT and states that service fees are non-refundable.

Every fee that can apply

  • Set-up fee: you "may be required to pay a one-time set-up fee". It may be waived in certain circumstances or promotional periods. The policy doesn't state an amount.
  • Listing fee: $0.20 per item, "whether or not the listed item sells," unless you create a private listing.
  • Transaction fee: 6.5% of your displayed price plus whatever you charge for shipping and gift wrapping. For US sellers, it doesn't apply to sales tax.
  • Payment processing: varies with the location of your bank account and is charged on the total sale, including tax and shipping.
  • Offsite Ads: if a buyer clicks an Etsy ad for your item and orders within 30 days, you pay a standard fee of 15%. Shops with $10,000 or more in sales over the prior 365 days pay 12%, and that participation is mandatory "for the lifetime of your shop". Shops under $10,000 can opt out. The fee won't exceed $100 per order.
  • Etsy Plus (optional): $10 per month.
  • Currency conversion: 2.5% if you list in a currency different from your payment account's.

Worked example (illustration)

Say you sell a $20 item and charge $5 for shipping, from a US shop. The 6.5% transaction fee applies to $25, which is about $1.63. Add the $0.20 listing fee and Etsy's fees reach about $1.83 before payment processing (fee rates per Etsy's policy). Payment processing comes on top, and the rate depends on your bank's country, so look up your own rate. If the sale came through Offsite Ads, add 15% or 12% of the order as well. This is arithmetic, not a forecast.

The catch: listings renew and charge again

Etsy.com listings expire after four months. With automatic renewal on, each renewal charges another $0.20 "until you cancel automatic renewals" (source). If you have dozens of slow-selling listings, review them before they renew.

Self-publishing ebooks with Kindle Direct Publishing

Amazon KDP lets you publish ebooks and earn a royalty on each sale. Ebooks are one of several digital products people build. For more ideas in that direction, see our roundup of passive income ideas.

35% vs 70% royalty, and when each applies

You choose one of two royalty options for each ebook: 35% or 70%. The 70% option pays 70% of your list price without VAT, less delivery costs, for sales to customers in the 70% territories. Sales to customers outside those territories earn 35%. You don't need to live in a 70% territory yourself.

Price matters too. According to KDP's list price requirements, from July 7, 2026, the 70% price band on Amazon.com is $2.99 to $12.99, up from $2.99 to $9.99. On the 35% option, the Amazon.com minimum depends on file size: $0.99 under 3 MB, $1.99 for 3 to 10 MB, and $2.99 for 10 MB or more. The maximum is $200.00.

Worked examples

Amazon's own example: a $0.99 ebook on the 35% option, sold to a buyer at 0% VAT, earns 0.35 × $0.99 = $0.35 (source).

Illustration: a $4.99 ebook on the 70% option, sold to a buyer in a 70% territory at 0% VAT, gives 0.70 × $4.99 = $3.49, minus delivery costs. KDP says average delivery costs are $0.06 per unit and vary by file size. With that average, the estimate is about $3.43 per sale. A larger file would cost more to deliver.

The catch: not every book or market qualifies for 70%

  • Works in the public domain, or made up mainly of public domain content, can only use the 35% option (source).
  • For sales to customers in Brazil, Japan, Mexico and India, the ebook must also be enrolled in KDP Select to qualify for 70% (source).
  • Delivery costs still apply on the 70% option.

Earning from YouTube

YouTube pays through the YouTube Partner Program (YPP), and it suits people willing to build an audience for months before any payout. YouTube states plainly that the partner agreement makes no promise about how much, or whether, you'll be paid.

Eligibility thresholds and review

Per YouTube's YPP eligibility page, you need to follow YouTube's monetization policies, live in a country or region where YPP is available, and have no active Community Guidelines strikes. You also need either:

  1. 1,000 subscribers with 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months, or
  2. 1,000 subscribers with 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days.

Watch hours from Shorts views in the Shorts Feed don't count toward the 4,000 hours. Meeting a threshold also doesn't get you in automatically: every channel that qualifies goes through a standard review (source).

Revenue shares by module

According to YouTube's partner earnings overview:

  • Watch Page Monetization: 55% of net revenue from ads on your public videos' watch pages.
  • Shorts Monetization: 45% of the revenue allocated to you based on your share of views from the Creator Pool.
  • Commerce Product: 70% of net revenue from channel memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers and Super Thanks.

When you get paid

Finalized earnings for the previous month are added to your balance between the 7th and 12th of the month. They're paid out by the 21st to 26th, as long as your balance has reached the payment threshold and you have no payment holds. YouTube requires U.S. tax info from all monetizing creators, wherever they live. If it isn't provided, Google may be required to withhold at the maximum rate (source).

The catches

  • Ads may appear on your videos before you join YPP, but until you qualify, you aren't eligible for a share of that revenue (source).
  • YouTube may turn off monetization on channels that haven't uploaded a video or posted to the Posts tab for 6 months or more.
  • YPP updates take effect on February 1, 2027. To keep fully monetizing, review and accept the updated terms in YouTube Studio by January 31, 2027. The menu may look different on your account.

Paid research studies (Prolific)

Prolific connects participants with researchers who need people for studies. It suits you if you want small, flexible tasks rather than building a business. The steps below come from Prolific's participant page:

  1. Join the waitlist. Prolific says this takes about 3 minutes, and it invites people "as soon as possible."
  2. Accept your invite.
  3. Verify your identity and complete a short practice task, which Prolific says takes about 15 minutes.
  4. Choose tasks. You can exit a task at any time, for any reason.

Once you've earned at least $6/£6, you can cash out to PayPal. After your first 4 cashouts, payments are transferred instantly. Prolific says you always receive cash, never gift cards or points (source). The page we read doesn't state pay rates, so check study details inside your account. How to spot an online job scam

Plenty of offers promise easy online income. The FTC's job scams guide lists clear warning signs:

  • Paying to get a job. "Honest employers, including the federal government, will never ask you to pay to get a job. Anyone who does is a scammer."
  • Starter kits and training fees. In work-from-home scams, people end up paying for starter kits, "so-called training," or certifications that are useless.
  • Fake check overpayments. No honest employer will send you a check to deposit and then ask you to send part of the money on, or buy gift cards with it.
  • Reshipping. "Reshipping goods is never a real job."
  • Big money, little work. An offer claiming you can make a lot of money in a short time with little work is "almost certainly a scam."

Before accepting an offer, search the company name with words like "scam," "review" or "complaint," and talk it over with someone you trust. The FTC notes that a lack of complaints "doesn't guarantee that a company is honest," but complaints can tip you off to problems (source). Report scams at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Taxes on online income (US)

Whichever method you choose, the money is taxable. The IRS rules below apply in the US. If you live elsewhere, the rules where you live will differ, so check with your local tax authority.

  • Report all of it. The IRS gig economy tax center says you must report gig income even if it's from part-time or side work, isn't reported on a form such as a 1099-K or 1099-NEC, or is paid in cash, property, goods or virtual currency. Its examples of gig work include selling goods online and providing creative or professional services.
  • The $400 filing rule. You must file a tax return if you have net earnings from self-employment of $400 or more from gig work, even if it's a side job (source).
  • Estimated taxes. Independent contractors may have to pay estimated taxes. The due dates are April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15, moving to the next business day if one falls on a weekend or legal holiday (source).
  • Forms. Self-employed people use Form 1040 with Schedule C and Schedule SE. Keep records of income and expenses so you can complete them.
  • Platform reporting. Etsy issues 1099-K forms to certain US sellers (source), and YouTube requires U.S. tax info from all monetizing creators (source).

How to choose your first method

There's no single best answer to how to make money online. Match the method to what you already have:

  • You have a skill clients pay for: start with freelancing, and budget for Connects.
  • You can make a product: try Etsy for physical or digital goods, or KDP for ebooks.
  • You'll build an audience for months before any payout: YouTube.
  • You want small, flexible tasks: research studies on Prolific.
  • Whatever you pick: do the after-fee maths before you start, using the platform's own fee page, and set aside money for tax.

FAQ

Is it free to start making money online?

Not always. Upwork Connects cost $0.15 each (source), and Etsy may charge a one-time set-up fee plus $0.20 per listing (source). Neither the KDP royalty pages nor YouTube's YPP pages we read list a joining fee.

How fast can I get paid?

On YouTube, finalized earnings are paid by the 21st to 26th of the following month, once you've passed the payment threshold (source). On Prolific, you can cash out once you reach $6/£6, and payouts become instant after your first 4 cashouts (source). The other platforms' payout timing isn't covered here.

Which online money-making offers are legit?

Use the FTC's tests: honest employers never ask you to pay to get a job, and offers of big money for little work are almost certainly scams (source). Search the company name with "scam" or "complaint" before you agree to anything.

Do I have to pay tax on small online earnings?

In the US, you must report gig income even without a 1099, and you must file if your net self-employment earnings are $400 or more (source).
