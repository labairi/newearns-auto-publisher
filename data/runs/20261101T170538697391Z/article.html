You can make money on Pinterest with a small account, but not from Pinterest itself. Pinterest doesn't pay creators directly today. Every method in this guide works the same way: a Pin sends someone to another site, and a merchant, a brand or your own shop pays you there.

That changes the questions worth asking. Instead of "how many followers do I need?", ask who pays, who takes a cut, what setup costs, and what you have to keep doing after a Pin goes live. This guide answers those questions for five methods, using Pinterest's, Amazon's, Etsy's and the FTC's own pages.

It won't promise an income or a timeline. None of the official sources give one, and any number you see elsewhere isn't a rule.

Does Pinterest pay creators?

Not directly. Pinterest's last direct payout program, Creator Rewards, paid creators for content built around monthly prompts and for reaching engagement goals. Pinterest said the program would end on November 30, 2022, as reported by TechCrunch.

The ways to earn that Pinterest describes now all involve someone else paying you:

  • Affiliate commissions: "Pinterest isn't part of the affiliate agreements," and your commission depends on the terms you set up with external merchants or programs (Pinterest Create).
  • Brand deals: you agree payment terms directly with the brand, and "Pinterest will not be directly involved in payment" (Pinterest Creators).

Pinterest's creator page also mentions a Creator Inclusion Fund focused on underrepresented creator communities. The page isn't dated, so check Pinterest's creator page to see whether it's currently open before planning around it.

If you're comparing platforms, this is the main difference from apps that run their own payout programs, such as the one covered in our guide to the TikTok Creator Rewards Program. On Pinterest, the money is always earned off the platform.

Before you start: set up a free business account

Pinterest says a free business account gives you access to analytics on your Pins and the ability to run ads (Pinterest Help). You need one to see Pinterest Analytics, which is how you'll find out which Pins send people to your links. You can create a new business account or convert your personal one.

Creating a new business account: Pinterest says you can currently only do this from a desktop device, and the email you sign up with must not already be in use on another Pinterest account (Pinterest Help).

Converting a personal account: business accounts are public, so a private personal account must be switched to public first. Pinterest says "Your Pins and followers will remain the same," and you can switch back to a personal account at any time (Pinterest Help).

  • On desktop: Settings → Account management → Convert to a business account → Convert account.
  • In the app: tap your profile picture → tap your profile photo → Account management → Convert to a business account → Convert account.

Menu names and positions can differ between app versions and devices. If a step doesn't match what you see, check the current help page.

Method 1: Link Pins to a site or offer you already monetize

This is the simplest route if you already have something that earns from visits: a blog with ads, a newsletter with paid tiers, a membership or a product page. Pinterest suggests driving people to pages "like subscriptions, membership sign-ups or even product pages" and making sure "all of your Pins include links to a web destination of your choosing" (Pinterest Create).

How to add the link:

  1. Start a new Pin and upload your image or video.
  2. While adding the descriptive details (title, board and so on), fill in the link field. Pinterest's help center says that's where the link option appears (Pinterest Help), and its creator page calls it the "finishing touches" editing page (Pinterest Creators).
  3. Link to the specific page that earns, not just your homepage. A Pin about a printable planner should go to the planner's sign-up or product page.

What isn't passive: the destination is yours to maintain. If the page breaks, moves or stops earning, the Pin stops helping. Pinterest also notes that over time your performance "may fluctuate or decrease" (Pinterest Help).

If you don't have a monetized site yet, our overview of how to make money online and what each platform takes covers options you could point Pins at.

Method 2: Affiliate links in your Pins

An affiliate link earns you a commission when someone clicks through and buys. Pinterest says your commission "will depend on the terms you've set up with external merchants or programs" (Pinterest Create).

How to add an affiliate link

  1. Join an affiliate program. Pinterest lists its official partners as Rakuten, LTK, ShopStyle and Amazon Associates US (Pinterest Create).
  2. Create a Pin by uploading an image or video.
  3. Paste your unique affiliate URL into the destination link, in place of a regular product URL. Pinterest notes the link "should go directly to a shop's product detail page."
  4. Match the Pin to the page. Pinterest asks that your Pin title and description match the information on the destination page.

Some link shorteners aren't supported. If your link gets blocked, Pinterest says you can edit it (Pinterest Create).

Pinterest's affiliate rules

Pinterest's Commercial and Branded Content Guidelines say you should generally operate only one Pinterest account, and that affiliate content "should be original and add unique value for Pinners." The guidelines prohibit:

  • Using fake accounts to create or save your affiliate links.
  • Asking other users to save your affiliate Pins in a quid pro quo.
  • Asking other users to create Pins with links that include your affiliate identifier.
  • Creating affiliate Pins "repetitively or in large volumes"; Pinterest asks marketers to use affiliate links in moderation.

Pinterest says it "may remove, limit or block the distribution of content or accounts" that break these guidelines (Pinterest Policy).

If you plan to use Amazon Associates

This is where a "without a big following" plan can run into trouble, so check it before you build around Amazon.

Pinterest names Amazon Associates US as an official affiliate partner (Pinterest Create). Separately, Amazon's application review page says it currently only accepts these social networks: Facebook (open group and fan pages, not personal pages), Instagram, Twitter, YouTube, TikTok and Twitch.tv. Pinterest isn't on that list. For social pages, Amazon looks for a substantive number of organic followers, "in most cases, at least 500" (Amazon Associates).

The same page says websites need "robust original content" (a rule of thumb of at least 10 posts) and recent content, generally within the last 60 days. If your setup is Pinterest-only, check Amazon's current requirements or contact Amazon before relying on it. A small blog that your Pins point to may fit Amazon's site criteria more clearly.

Other Amazon conditions to know:

  • Three sales in 180 days: Amazon checks your application once you've driven at least three qualified sales within the first 180 days, and personal orders don't count (Amazon Associates). If there aren't enough qualifying sales in that window, the application can be withdrawn, and Amazon says it can't reinstate an account or Associates ID after rejection (Amazon Associates).
  • Required statement: you must clearly and prominently state, "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases" (Amazon Associates Operating Agreement).
  • No income promise: Amazon says it does not make any representation about "the amount of traffic or commission income you can expect" (Operating Agreement).
  • When a sale counts: the product must be shipped to (or streamed or downloaded by) and paid for by the customer within 180 days of the purchase (Amazon Associates Program Policies).

Method 3: Product tags

Product tags link specific products in the "Shop the Look" section below your Pin. Pinterest suggests that combining product tags with affiliate links can add more chances to earn affiliate revenue per Pin (Pinterest Create). Example: a Pin of a styled desk setup could tag the lamp, chair and organizer separately.

Steps, per Pinterest's blog: when you create a Pin, select "tag products," then click the plus icon and either search for products by name or click "Use a link" (Pinterest Create). Pinterest's creator page refers to this as a "product" sticker, so the label you see may differ.

  • You can add up to five product tags per Pin, and tags must follow merchant and affiliate guidelines (Pinterest Help).
  • Product tagging is available in the US, CA, UK, DE, FR, IT, ES, AT, CH, NL, AU, BR, MX and JP (Pinterest Create). If you're elsewhere, you may not see the option.

Method 4: Paid brand partnerships, where audience size matters

This is the method where your numbers count. Pinterest doesn't connect you with brands or pay you here; the partnership is set up between you and the brand outside Pinterest (Pinterest Help).

Pinterest suggests pitching brands that fit your content with a media kit that includes your Pinterest profile, key metrics (Pin performance and audience insights), and sample content or past collaborations (Pinterest Creators). With a small account, sample work and strong metrics on a few Pins may carry more of the pitch than follower count.

How to add the paid partnership label

You need a free business profile, and the tool is only available in the Pinterest app (Pinterest Help). The steps Pinterest gives:

  1. Tap the plus icon, then Pin.
  2. Choose your media, tap Next, edit if needed, and tap Next.
  3. Fill in the Pin's details.
  4. Next to Advanced settings, tap the arrow icon.
  5. Toggle on "Add paid partnership label."
  6. Tap "Add a brand partner" and choose the brand (one brand per Pin).
  7. Go back, review your Pin and tap Create to publish.

What to know before you publish (Pinterest Help):

  • The brand approves or declines the tag. If it declines, the label is removed and you can't tag that brand again in the same Pin.
  • You can't add the label after a Pin is published.
  • If the brand isn't on Pinterest, you can still use the label, but the brand's name won't appear.
  • Creators can't promote these Pins as ads; only brand partners can. Pinterest notes some options may only be available to account owners above a certain age.

Method 5: Selling your own products through Pinterest

If you sell physical or digital products, Pins can point to your product pages. Pinterest doesn't charge you for that traffic, but the place you sell will have its own fees. Here's what Etsy's Fees & Payments Policy lists:

  • Set-up fee: you may be required to pay a one-time fee to open a shop.
  • Listing fee: $0.20 USD per item listed.
  • Expiry and renewal: listings expire after four months and automatically renew at $0.20 unless you cancel automatic renewals.
  • Transaction fee: 6.5% of the price you display, plus the amount you charge for shipping and gift wrapping.
  • Payment processing: varies based on the location of your bank account; check Etsy's policy pages for your rate.

Don't confuse your Pins with Etsy's Offsite Ads

Etsy's Offsite Ads fee applies when Etsy buys advertising on partners such as social networks and search engines, your listing appears in that ad, and a buyer orders from your shop within 30 days of clicking it. Shops under $10,000 a year pay 15% on those orders unless they opt out. Shops at $10,000 or more pay 12% for the lifetime of the shop and can no longer opt out (Etsy). This fee is about Etsy's own ads, not Pins you post yourself.

Disclosing affiliate links and sponsored Pins

The FTC's Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers says to disclose any financial, employment, personal or family relationship with a brand, and that "tags, likes, pins, and similar ways of showing you like a brand or product can be endorsements." Its guidance for placement and wording:

  • Put the disclosure with the endorsement message itself, not only on a profile page, at the end of a post, or anywhere people must click "more."
  • Don't mix it into a group of hashtags or links.
  • If the endorsement is in a picture, superimpose the disclosure over the picture.
  • Use clear terms like "ad," "advertisement" or "sponsored," not vague ones like "sp," "spon" or "collab."
  • Don't assume a platform's disclosure tool is good enough; consider using it in addition to your own disclosure.
  • If you post from abroad, U.S. law applies if it's reasonably foreseeable the post will affect U.S. consumers.

Pinterest's own rules say all paid partnerships must disclose their commercial nature, for example with #ad or the paid partnership label. If you live in the European Economic Area, commercial content must include a clear disclosure, and you must use any functionality Pinterest makes available for it (Pinterest Policy).

In practice, for an affiliate Pin: put a short line such as "Ad: I earn from purchases through this link" at the start of the description, and add text on the image itself if the image is the endorsement. Amazon affiliates also need Amazon's required statement.

How long before it earns, and what keeps it going

No source we used gives a typical time before Pinterest income starts, so this guide doesn't offer one. These are the only clocks the official pages set:

  • Amazon: 180 days after applying to make at least three qualified sales (Amazon Associates), and a 180-day window for an order to be shipped and paid for to count (Program Policies).
  • Amazon dormancy: if you've earned no commission in the previous three years, Amazon may charge an account maintenance fee of the lesser of $10 or your unpaid commission (Amazon Associates).
  • Etsy: listings expire after four months and auto-renew at $0.20 unless you turn that off (Etsy).
  • Pinterest Analytics: outbound clicks are measured starting from the date the link is added (Pinterest Help), so adding links to older Pins won't show past clicks.

Ongoing upkeep:

  • Check links regularly, especially affiliate links to products that may sell out or move.
  • After publishing, you can edit a Pin's title, board and some details, but not its image, video or audio (Pinterest Help). Fix a broken link by editing; replace a dated image with a new Pin.
  • Post affiliate Pins in moderation. Pinterest says spam-related behavior can get an account temporarily blocked (Pinterest Help).

Track which Pins earn

Pinterest Analytics requires a business account (Pinterest Help). On desktop, click the hamburger icon, then under Analytics select Overview. Interface details may differ on your device.

The metric closest to income is outbound clicks: the number of times people take actions that lead them to a destination off Pinterest. Look at it alongside impressions, saves and Pin clicks. Pinterest notes that real-time metrics are estimates and may decrease, and you can view custom date ranges for the past year and a half (Pinterest Help).

A simple routine: once a month, list your Pins with the most outbound clicks, then compare those against sales in your affiliate dashboard or shop. Make more Pins like the ones that lead to sales, not just the ones with the most impressions. FAQ

Can you make money on Pinterest without followers?

The Pinterest pages we read don't set a follower minimum for adding links, affiliate links or the paid partnership label. Follower and engagement numbers matter most for brand deals, where Pinterest suggests showing your metrics in a media kit (Pinterest Creators). Individual affiliate programs can set their own requirements, as Amazon does.

Does Pinterest pay for views?

No current program does. Creator Rewards, which paid for content and engagement goals, ended on November 30, 2022 (TechCrunch). Earnings now come from merchants, brands or your own sales off Pinterest.

Do I need a blog?

Not for Pinterest's link or affiliate features: affiliate links can go directly to a shop's product page (Pinterest Create). Some affiliate programs, though, review your website or social presence. Amazon's application page lists site criteria and doesn't include Pinterest among accepted social networks (Amazon Associates).

Can I use Amazon affiliate links on Pinterest?

Pinterest lists Amazon Associates US as an official partner. Amazon's own application criteria don't list Pinterest as an accepted social network, so check Amazon's current rules for your situation before relying on a Pinterest-only setup.
