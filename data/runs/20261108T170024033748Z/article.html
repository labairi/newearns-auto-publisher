Most side-hustle lists stop at the idea. If you need cash this month, three practical questions matter more: how quickly you can get approved, what it costs to start, and how many days pass between finishing the work and seeing the money in your bank account.

This guide explains how to make extra money on a short timeline in the US. It covers selling things you already own, delivering food, doing local tasks and checking for money you're already owed. Requirements, fees and payout timing come from each platform's own help pages. It doesn't estimate earnings, because those depend on where you live, the hours you work and local demand. One caveat applies throughout. Apps, screens and terms change, and some rules vary by city or state. Treat the details below as what each platform's pages said when this guide was researched, and check the current terms in the app before you sign up.

Sell things you already own

Selling is often the quickest start, because there's no approval step and nothing to buy first. The fees and the timing of your first payout are what decide how fast the cash arrives.

What eBay charges when an item sells

The figures below are for most categories, according to eBay's selling fees page. Some categories differ. Books, movies and music, for example, have a final value fee of 15.3%.

FeeAmount (most categories)
Insertion feeNone for up to 250 listings a month (more with an eBay Store), then $0.35 per listing
Final value fee13.6% of the total sale up to $7,500 per item, plus 2.35% on the portion over $7,500
Per-order fee$0.30 for orders of $10 or less; $0.40 for orders over $10

eBay calculates the final value fee on the total amount of the sale. That includes the item price, handling, the shipping you charge the buyer (with some exceptions) and sales tax. Here's a worked example using those published rates. A buyer pays $40 for the item, $6 for shipping and $4 in sales tax, a $50 total. The final value fee is 13.6% of $50, which is $6.80. Add the $0.40 per-order fee and the fees come to $7.20. When you set a price, remember the percentage applies to shipping and tax as well.

When the money reaches you as a new seller

According to eBay's getting-paid page, a payment moves through these stages:

  1. The buyer pays, and eBay confirms the payment.
  2. Your proceeds usually become "Available for payout" 1–2 days later, unless the sale is on hold.
  3. On daily payouts, eBay usually starts the payout 2 days after confirming the buyer's payment. On weekly payouts, it starts them on Tuesday for the previous Monday to Sunday.
  4. The funds then usually take another 1–3 business days to clear. Holidays push payouts to the next payout day.

If you need the money sooner, an express payout is usually available within 30 minutes. eBay charges a flat $2.00 for each express payout sent to your linked debit card or bank account.

New private sellers have one more step to plan for. Until they build a consistent track record, eBay's payment holds page says they usually get their funds:

  • within 2 days after delivery confirmation if they ship with an eBay label;
  • 7 days after delivery confirmation if they use a third-party label;
  • 31 days after the buyer pays if the item ships without tracking.

For your first sales, an eBay shipping label gives you the shortest wait under these rules. Shipping without tracking can push your money into next month.

Selling locally

eBay also offers local pickup. When you scan or enter the buyer's pickup code, that confirms delivery, which is the event the new-seller holds are counted from.

Facebook Marketplace is another common choice for local sales. This guide couldn't find an official Facebook page that sets out Marketplace selling fees, and third-party sites disagree, so no figure is given here. Before you list or ship, check the fees and terms Facebook shows you at the time.

Deliver food with DoorDash or Uber Eats

Who can sign up

DoorDash. DoorDash's requirements page says you must be at least 18, have an iPhone or Android smartphone, and complete sign-up. The minimum age is 21 for new applicants in California. It is 19 in Arizona, Colorado, Delaware, Florida, Georgia, Idaho, Kentucky, Montana, New Jersey, New Mexico, Texas, Utah and West Virginia. To deliver by car you need a valid driver's licence and personal auto insurance. Bikes, e-bikes and scooters may be allowed, depending on your location.

Uber Eats. Uber's delivery page sets requirements by how you deliver:

  • Bike or foot: 18+, a government ID and an SSN for screening.
  • Scooter: 19+, a valid licence and an SSN.
  • Car: 19+ (21 for car drivers in California and New York State) and a 2- or 4-door car with valid insurance.

Uber also says delivery options vary by city and that many markets require proof of residency. Third parties run the background check, and Uber's help page says it typically takes 3–5 business days. Build that wait into your plan if you're counting on this month.

How pay works and how to cash out faster

DoorDash says Dasher pay is base pay plus 100% of customer tips plus promotions. In Earn per Offer mode, DoorDash says base pay ranges from $2–10+ depending on the offer's estimated time, distance and desirability. That's the base pay for a single offer. It isn't a measure of what you'll earn per hour or per week.

DoorDash offers three ways to get paid:

  • Weekly direct deposit: usually takes 2–3 days to show up.
  • Fast Pay: $1.99, once a day, to a linked debit card (prepaid cards don't work). Transfers usually take minutes but can take a few business days. Fast Pay isn't available to all Dashers. Earnings you haven't cashed out by Sunday 11:59:59 PM PST go into the weekly deposit.
  • DoorDash Crimson: a bank account from Starion Bank that offers instant deposits with no fee. DoorDash notes that deposits may be delayed or not completed.

On Uber Eats, couriers keep 100% of tips. You can take weekly automatic transfers, or use Instant Pay to cash out up to 6 times a day for a fee. Uber's page doesn't give the fee amount, so check it in the app before relying on it.

Costs that don't show on the pay screen

DoorDash requires Dashers to keep their own insurance, and says that without it DoorDash's coverage may not apply. Pay screens show what you're paid, not what you spent to get there. Self-employment tax is figured on net earnings, so keep a simple record of what you spend to do the work from your first day.

Do local jobs on Taskrabbit

If you can assemble furniture, mount shelves or help with a move, Taskrabbit connects you with local clients. Taskrabbit's requirements page lists:

  • age 18 or over;
  • a US Social Security number, which is used for the background check;
  • a background and ID check;
  • working in an active US city;
  • a checking account (savings accounts and prepaid cards won't work);
  • a smartphone;
  • a valid credit card for a one-time, non-refundable $25 registration fee.

Taskrabbit describes three earning structures. Task-Based pays a fixed amount per task, such as IKEA assembly. Pre-Set Hourly applies to direct hires. Under Self-Set Hourly, Taskrabbit says you get 100% of the rate you set plus 100% of tips. That 100% statement applies to Self-Set Hourly only. Most payouts arrive within 1–3 business days through Stripe.

Clients pay the Taskrabbit Service Fee on top of the Tasker's rate. Before you pay the $25, confirm your city is active and that you have time to take tasks this month.

Check whether you're already owed money

This one costs nothing and takes minutes, and lists of side hustles rarely mention it. According to USA.gov, states hold most unclaimed money. Search your current state's unclaimed property office and those of any states you've lived in. USA.gov also says there is no single place to look for all unclaimed money. It points to separate federal databases for:

  • back wages owed by employers (Department of Labor);
  • pensions (Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation);
  • savings bonds (TreasuryHunt);
  • deposits from failed banks (FDIC);
  • unclaimed funds held by the US courts.

Use the links from the USA.gov page so you land on official sites. You may find nothing. If you do find something, it's money that was already yours, not new income.

Taxes on extra income: what the IRS says

  • Report it all. The IRS says you must report gig economy income on a tax return. That applies even if the work is part-time, you don't receive an information return, or you're paid in cash, property or virtual currency.
  • Form 1099-K. Payment apps and online marketplaces must send a Form 1099-K when payments total over $20,000 across more than 200 transactions, and they may send one for less. Whether or not you get the form, you must still report your income.
  • Selling personal items. The same IRS page says personal items sold at a gain must be reported. Gifts and repayments from friends and family aren't taxable income.
  • Self-employment tax. You usually must pay it if your net earnings from self-employment are $400 or more. The rate is 12.4% for Social Security plus 2.9% for Medicare, and it generally applies to 92.35% of net earnings. Half of it is deductible when you figure your adjusted gross income.

Because self-employment tax is based on net earnings, an income and expense record is worth starting now. Don't wait until tax season.

Red flags: "jobs" that ask you for money

When people search for quick income, fake recruiters are often waiting for them. In an April 2026 consumer alert, the FTC described fake recruiters who text about remote jobs. Warning signs include:

  • a text asking you to reply "YES" to learn more;
  • a check sent to you, with a request to send some of the money back (the check is fake);
  • "task scams" that pay small amounts for rating products or apps, then ask you to deposit your own money to keep going.

The FTC's advice is to ignore unexpected job texts and WhatsApp or Telegram messages and never pay to get a job. In its words: "Never pay to get paid or get a job. That's a sure sign of a scam." Report suspected scams at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. For more on spotting fakes, see data entry jobs from home and how to spot scams.

A simple plan for your first week

The timings below are the typical times each platform states. Real waits can be longer.

  1. Day 1: search for unclaimed money. Check every state you've lived in, starting from USA.gov.
  2. Day 1–2: list items you don't need. Use eBay labels or tracked shipping, so a new-seller hold is counted from delivery, not a 31-day wait.
  3. Day 1–2: apply to one delivery app. Allow for the background check, which Uber says typically takes 3–5 business days. Applying to one app at a time keeps your paperwork manageable.
  4. Decide on Taskrabbit. If you have a practical skill and live in an active city, weigh the non-refundable $25 fee against the time you can give it this month.
  5. Set up payouts. Link a checking account, and a debit card if you plan to use a faster cash-out option. Remember that faster usually costs money: $1.99 for DoorDash Fast Pay, for example.
  6. Start a record. Use a basic spreadsheet with the date, platform, amount received, fees and expenses. It will help you work out net earnings at tax time.

FAQ

Do I pay tax on selling my old stuff?

The IRS says you must report personal items sold at a gain, and that you must report income whether or not you receive a Form 1099-K. The same page says gifts and repayments from friends and family aren't taxable income.

What's the fastest way to get paid?

Of the options here, the platforms describe these as their quickest routes. An eBay express payout is usually available within 30 minutes for $2.00, but new-seller holds can still delay when funds become available. DoorDash Fast Pay usually takes minutes, for $1.99, if you're eligible. Uber Eats Instant Pay lets you cash out up to 6 times a day for a fee. None of these speeds up approval, so the background check still comes first for delivery work.

Can I get paid into a savings account or PayPal?

Not on eBay or Taskrabbit. eBay says savings and PayPal accounts can't receive payouts, and Taskrabbit says savings accounts and prepaid cards won't work. Both require a checking account.

Is the Taskrabbit registration fee refundable?

No. Taskrabbit describes it as a one-time, non-refundable $25 registration fee.
