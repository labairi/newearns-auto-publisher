With print on demand, you upload a design and list the product. A print company makes it only after a customer orders, as Printify's pricing page describes the model. This print on demand for beginners guide adds up who takes a cut of each sale, what setup costs, and which parts of the work keep coming back. It does not promise any income.

If you found print on demand on a list of passive income ideas, read the section on what isn't passive before you open any accounts. All prices below were checked against each vendor's own pricing and policy pages at the time of writing. They can change, so confirm them before you sign up.

How print on demand works, and who gets paid

You set the price, the print company charges a base price, the store takes fees

Three parties share each sale. You choose the retail price. The print company charges you its cost to make and ship the item. The marketplace or store platform takes its fees. Printful states its formula simply: profit = retail price − Printful cost. That formula is accurate for what Printful charges, but it stops there. If you sell on Etsy or Shopify, their fees come out of the same sale. The worked example below shows how much difference they make.

What the base price covers, and what it doesn't

According to Printful's pricing page, its base price covers the blank product, one print or embroidery placement, and fulfillment. These cost extra:

  • Additional print placements. Each extra embroidery placement is $2.95 (Printful pricing).
  • A one-time embroidery digitization fee, Getty images, labels and packaging (Printful pricing).
  • Shipping, which depends on the product, order size and destination, and tax, which is based on the customer's location (Printful pricing).

Printful also notes that prices may vary slightly by fulfillment location. Printify's profit calculator is similar: its cost figure "doesn't include shipping fees, taxes, and other possible storefront expenses" (Printify pricing).

What it costs to set up

Print company accounts

Both big print companies let you start without a monthly fee:

  • Printify: a $0 plan with up to 5 stores, or Premium from $39/month billed monthly, with up to 10 stores (Printify pricing). The pricing page shows "from $24.99" a month when billed yearly. The help center lists the yearly price as USD 299 and says yearly billing saves "up to 36%" (Printify Premium help). US and Canadian prices have tax added on top.
  • Printful: a Free plan at $0/month with no upfront or monthly fees, or Growth at $24.99/month with "up to 33% off product pricing." Growth "becomes free once you reach $12K/year in sales" (Printful pricing).

You don't need a paid plan to make your first sale. Whether one is worth it later is a simple sum, covered further down.

Etsy

Etsy's Fees & Payments Policy says you "may be required" to pay a one-time set-up fee. The amount is shown during shop setup and may be waived, and the policy doesn't state a figure. After that, each listing costs $0.20, whether or not the item sells (private listings are the exception). Twenty designs listed once costs $4.00 in listing fees before you sell anything. Etsy Plus ($10/month) and Pattern ($15/month after a 30-day trial) are optional extras (Etsy fees).

Your own Shopify store

On Shopify's US pricing page, the Basic plan is $39 USD/month paid monthly, or $29 USD/month paid yearly. Shopify says there are "no setup fees" and that it "starts with a free 3-day trial—no credit card required." Prices can differ by country, so check the pricing page for your region. Shopify also runs introductory promotions on most plans, but these change, so don't count on one when you budget.

Samples

Ordering a sample lets you check print quality and sizing before customers do. Printful gives 20% off sample orders on the Free plan and 25% off on Growth, limited to 1 sample order a month of up to 3 items (Printful pricing). Budget for samples as a real setup cost.

Etsy Offsite Ads: 15% or 12%, and when you can opt out

Etsy may advertise your listings on other websites. If a buyer clicks one of those ads and orders within 30 days, you pay an Offsite Ads fee. The standard rate is 15%. Shops with less than $10,000 in sales over the prior 365 days can opt out. Once a shop reaches $10,000 or more, the rate drops to 12% for the shop's lifetime, but opting out is no longer allowed. The fee is capped at $100 USD per order. If the buyer's last click was an Etsy Ad, only the Etsy Ads fee applies (Etsy fees). For a $35 tee, the 15% fee is larger than the transaction and processing fees combined, so decide early whether to stay opted in.

Why the print companies' calculators show more than you'll take home

Printify's calculator example shows "$813.13" a month for the $8.77 tee sold at $35 with one sale a day (Printify pricing). That is exactly ($35 − $8.77) × 31 days, which makes it a gross margin. Printify says on the page that the cost excludes shipping, taxes and storefront expenses, so the calculator works as designed. It just answers a narrower question than "what will I keep?" On Printify's own assumption of one sale a day, taking off the $3.78 of Etsy fees from each sale leaves about $695.95 for 31 sales. That is still before shipping, tax, ads and returns. It illustrates the arithmetic, and it is not a forecast of sales.

Do the paid plans pay for themselves?

A paid plan is worth it only when the discount on what you sell adds up to more than the fee. The formula is:

Items per month to break even = monthly plan fee ÷ (base cost × discount %)

Printify Premium, at the maximum discount. Premium gives up to 20% off most products, and up to 33% on select new products (Printify Premium help). On the $8.77 tee, 20% is about $1.75 per item. At $39/month you would need about 22 items a month (22.2, so the 23rd item puts you ahead). On the yearly price of USD 299 (about $24.92 a month), it's about 14 items (14.2). These are best-case numbers, because the discount is "up to" and real discounts vary by product. They are not typical results.

Printful Growth. Use the same formula with $24.99 as the fee and your product's base price. The discount is "up to 33%," so the result is a best case, and Growth becomes free once you reach $12K/year in sales (Printful pricing).

Read the cancellation terms before you upgrade. Printify says "The Premium plan subscription fee is non-refundable," and you need to unsubscribe before the renewal date to avoid the next charge. Uninstalling the Printify app from Shopify cancels Premium, but uninstalling it from other sales channels does not (Printify Premium help). Printful's Growth plan can be cancelled anytime, and its benefits last until the end of the billing cycle (Printful pricing).

The parts of print on demand that aren't passive

The print company handles production and shipping. Everything else stays with you, every month:

  • Wrong size and change of mind are your cost. Printify says returns and exchanges "are not supported if your customer ordered the wrong size, color, or simply changed their mind" (Printify refunds help). Printful says "we don't accept returns or exchanges for buyer's remorse, wrong size, or wrong color" (Printful returns policy). If you refund a buyer anyway, Etsy recalculates the processing fee on the adjusted sale price (Etsy Payments Policy), and Etsy states that its service fees are non-refundable (Etsy fees).
  • Misprint and damage claims have 30-day deadlines. Printify offers a free reprint or refund for damage or manufacturing errors if you contact it within 30 calendar days of delivery with a photo. It allows a 0.5" tolerance on print placement for DTG, DTF, EMB and AOP products, so small shifts don't count as defects (Printify refunds help). Printful's window is 30 days from receiving the item for damage or misprints, and 30 days from the estimated delivery date for lost packages (Printful claim window). Later claims "can't be honored" (Printful returns policy). Someone has to collect the buyer's photos and file each claim on time.
  • Customer mistakes still reach you. If a customer enters the wrong address, Printful can reship the order, but reshipping costs apply. Unclaimed returned packages are donated after 30 days (Printful returns policy). The buyer will contact you, not the print company, so customer service stays your job.
  • Listings renew and cost money. Etsy listings expire after four months and auto-renew at $0.20 each until you switch renewal to "Manual" in Listings Manager. Multi-quantity listings renew at $0.20 after each item sells (Etsy fees). Review your listings regularly and drop designs that don't sell.
  • Production partner disclosure on every listing. Etsy's rule is that "Sellers must disclose that an item is made by a production partner, and provide accurate information about where the item will ship from" (Etsy Creativity Standards). Printful says it doesn't add itself automatically. You add it under Shop Manager → Settings → Partners you work with → Add production partner. Then, in each listing, under About this listing, you set "Who made it?" to "Another company or person" and choose Printful. You can show a generic term like "A print shop" publicly, and skipping this step may lead Etsy to block or delay orders (Printful help). Etsy's menus may look different from this description, so look for the equivalent settings if the labels have changed.
  • AI disclosure. Etsy requires you to say in the listing description if AI was used (Etsy Creativity Standards). If you make designs with tools like those in our guide to free AI tools and what each free plan includes, add the disclosure to every affected listing. AI prompt bundles don't qualify for sale under the same standards.
  • Copyright and trademark risk. Etsy says "Sellers are responsible for ensuring they have all necessary rights to their content." It terminates selling privileges for repeat or multiple infringement notices "in appropriate circumstances and at Etsy's discretion," and it can refuse service to new shops linked to the same person (Etsy IP Policy). Check every design before you list it, not after a complaint arrives.

How long before it earns?

None of the vendor pages used for this guide gives a timeline, and this guide won't invent one. What can be said is what has to happen before the first order arrives. You create designs you have the rights to. You order samples and check them. You set prices that cover the costs above, including your shipping rate. You publish listings with the right disclosures. Then buyers have to find those listings. That last step is the one you control least. If you want a realistic view of online earnings in general, read Can You Really Make $100 a Day Online? Beginner checklist

  1. Open a free print company account, such as Printify's $0 plan or Printful's Free plan. Wait on paid plans until the break-even formula says they pay off.
  2. Choose where to sell: Etsy (low setup cost, fees on every sale) or your own Shopify store (monthly fee, you find the customers).
  3. Order samples of each product type before listing it.
  4. Build your price from the full cost: base price + shipping + every platform fee + an allowance for refunds you choose to absorb.
  5. Decide whether to opt out of Etsy Offsite Ads while your shop is under the $10,000 threshold.
  6. On Etsy, add your production partner and set "Who made it?" on every listing. Disclose AI use where it applies.
  7. Check each design for copyright and trademark problems before publishing.
  8. Put the 30-day claim deadlines and Etsy's four-month listing renewals in your calendar.
  9. Re-check vendor pricing pages before you upgrade or change plans.

Print on demand removes the need to hold stock, but it doesn't remove the fees or the upkeep. Do the sums for your own product first, and treat any income as something you build, not something you are promised.

FAQ

Do I need money to start print on demand?

Not much. Printify and Printful both have $0 plans (Printify pricing, Printful pricing). On Etsy you pay $0.20 per listing and possibly a one-time set-up fee shown at setup (Etsy fees). Samples are a real cost too, so plan for them.

Is print on demand good for beginners?

It has low upfront costs, which is why it suits a beginner's budget. But the margins on each sale shrink after platform fees, and the ongoing work never goes away. Use the worked example above with your own product and shipping rate before deciding.

Do I have to tell Etsy I use Printful or Printify?

Yes. Etsy requires sellers to disclose when an item is made by a production partner (Etsy Creativity Standards). Printful's help center explains how to add it on each listing (Printful help).

Can I sell AI-generated designs on Etsy?

Etsy's Creativity Standards require you to disclose AI use in the listing description (Etsy Creativity Standards). You also remain responsible for having the rights to your content (Etsy IP Policy).

Who pays when a customer orders the wrong size?

Neither Printify nor Printful accepts returns for wrong size, wrong color or change of mind (Printify refunds help, Printful returns policy). Any refund you offer the buyer comes out of your own margin.
