How much money can you make donating plasma? It depends on things the big centers state openly. There is a per-visit fee that varies by center and by your body measurements. There are temporary bonuses for new donors. And there are offers that change from week to week. No honest guide can promise you a number. This one shows what CSL Plasma, BioLife and Octapharma Plasma publish, how often federal rules let you donate, and how to estimate your own monthly ceiling and hourly rate.

Why your pay per visit differs from someone else's

Two people at the same center on the same day can be paid different amounts. The centers name several reasons.

Your weight, height and hematocrit

Octapharma's FAQ says: “Payment for your plasma donation time is based on a combination of weight, height, and hematocrit.” Its earning-potential page explains that hematocrit “measures the proportion of red blood cells in your blood and affects how much plasma you can donate.” It adds that “generally, donors with higher plasma supply can donate more, which could result in higher payouts.” The same page describes “a tiered payment system that rewards donors for their dedication and consistency.”

Location, donation history and market conditions

BioLife says compensation per donation “may change over time and may vary based on factors including your donation history, location, and market conditions.” Octapharma says something similar in its FAQ: donor fees “may vary month-to-month, by location, or even for special promotions.” That FAQ also says pay is based on “the time it takes you to donate and other factors.”

Promotions end, and pay goes back to the normal schedule

Many donors notice their pay drop after the first few weeks. Octapharma's FAQ answers “Why did donor payments go down?” this way: payments “are not decreased but rather returning to the normal payment schedule when the promotional period has expired.” Plan around the regular rate, not the new-donor rate.

BioLife's weekly offer cycle

At BioLife, “the donor payment schedule runs from Monday to Sunday. Each week, you'll see the latest offer, which will expire at 11:59 p.m. on Sunday,” according to its promotions and payments guide. So an amount you saw last week may not be what you are offered this week.

How often you can donate, and the most visits you can fit in a month

The frequency limit is set by federal regulation. Under 21 CFR 640.65(b)(8), automated plasma collection “shall not occur less than 2 days apart or more frequently than twice in a 7-day period.”

The centers put the same rule in plainer words:

  • Octapharma: “You can donate up to two times per week, provided you remain eligible and healthy, with at least 2 days between donations” (New Donors page).
  • BioLife: “up to two times within seven days, as long as you wait 48 hours between donations” (donor compensation guide).
  • CSL Plasma: “FDA guidelines allow people to donate up to twice a week” (first-time donor guide).

Our calculation (not a figure published by any center): if you donate twice a week on a schedule such as days 1, 4, 8, 11, 15, 18, 22, 25 and 29, a 30-day month allows about 8 to 9 visits. That is a maximum. Missed days, travel, illness or being turned away at screening will lower it. To estimate your own monthly ceiling, use this formula:

  • Monthly ceiling = (number of visits you can realistically make) × (your center's regular per-visit fee) + (any promotion you actually complete).

As a reference point, BioLife's loyalty program lists “donating seven times in one calendar month” as one of its milestones.

To work out your hourly rate, use this formula: per-visit pay ÷ (visit time + round-trip travel time). Example only, using made-up numbers to show the math rather than any center's rate: if a visit paid $40 and took 1.5 hours, plus 30 minutes of driving, you would earn $40 ÷ 2 hours = $20 an hour, before any fuel or parking costs. Plug in your own center's current fee and your own travel time.

How the payment actually reaches you

All three chains pay onto a card, not in cash.

  • BioLife: compensation “is automatically loaded onto a Mastercard” for every donation (compensation page). BioLife says you'll “never be asked for a credit check.” You must activate the card before it can transfer money to you. You can withdraw cash at ATMs or transfer payments to your bank account (promotions and payments guide). Checking your balance is free through the cardholder dashboard or by texting BAL to 445544. Checking it at an ATM costs a fee.
  • Octapharma: the prepaid card “can be used free of fees for both credit and debit purchases.” Both of its card providers, Northlane and Comdata, “charge $1.50 fee for ATM withdrawals” (FAQ). For the full list of fees, see the card insert that comes with your card.
  • CSL Plasma: “You'll receive your payment on a prepaid card after your first donation” (homepage).

The practical point: ATM withdrawals and ATM balance checks can eat into small payments. Where your card allows it, use fee-free purchases or check balances online. Read your card's fee schedule before moving money to a bank account.

The catches most pay charts skip

You have to come back for a second visit

Octapharma says you “need to come back and donate 2 to 14 days after your first visit.” Your first donation can't be used to produce medicines until after the second one (FAQ). CSL says the same: “We can't use your first donation until your second one is complete” (Start Donating).

New-donor promotions need several visits in a set window

BioLife says promotional offers “generally require donors to make a specific number of donations within 30 days.” If you don't finish, “you'll still get to keep the bonus rates paid for the donations you did successfully complete” (BioLife). Octapharma's new-donor payments apply during your first 35 days. If you can't commit to regular visits in that window, don't count on the headline figure.

Screening can stop you on the day

CSL says that “if screening shows that donating may not be safe for you, you won't be allowed to donate that day” (CSL). Federal rules require centers to weigh you at each donation. They must also check your total plasma protein before each procedure, which must be between 6.0 and 9.0 grams per deciliter (21 CFR 630.15; text as printed in the 2022 CFR edition). The same rule requires a new medical history and physical exam for a donor who does not return for 6 months. At return visits, Octapharma's FAQ says it checks red blood cell proportion, protein, blood pressure, pulse, temperature and weight. A wasted trip costs you time and travel, with no pay.

Paperwork and where you can donate

Octapharma asks for “a valid, government-issued photo ID, proof of your Social Security Number, and proof of your current address (dated within the last 60 days)” (New Donors). CSL asks for a photo ID, proof of Social Security and proof of residency or a current lease. It also says to donate at “a center within your recruitment area.” CSL notes “No appointment necessary” (CSL homepage).

Referral bonuses have conditions

BioLife's Buddy Bonus pays you extra only when the person you refer completes “two successful donations” (BioLife). CSL advertises “Earn for every referral” on its homepage. Ask your center for the current terms.

Is plasma money taxable?

The centers do not give a simple yes or no. BioLife says it “follows all reporting requirements for plasma donation fees under the current U.S. healthcare law. As a donor, you won't receive any tax documents from BioLife” (BioLife). Octapharma's answer is: “Please reach out to a local tax expert for further guidance” (Octapharma).

The IRS's general rule, in Publication 525, is: “In most cases, an amount included in your income is taxable unless it is specifically exempted by law.” The IRS pages reviewed for this guide do not mention plasma donation specifically. The safe approach is to keep your own record of every payment, for example from your card or app payment history, and ask a tax professional how to treat it. Who can donate: basic eligibility

  • Age: at least 18 at CSL (Start Donating). Octapharma's range is 18 to 70 (New Donors).
  • Weight: at least 110 pounds at both chains. The federal rule sets a minimum of 50 kilograms (110 pounds) (21 CFR 630.15(a)(5)).
  • Health: CSL asks that you “be in good health.”
  • Tattoos and piercings: CSL requires none “within the last four months.”
  • ID and address: CSL asks for a valid ID and permanent address.

Every center runs its own screening, so meeting these basics does not mean you will be accepted on a given day.

Getting started this week: a checklist

  1. Find a nearby center and check its current offer. Look for the new-donor amount, how many visits it requires, and the deadline (30 days at BioLife for most promotions, 35 days at Octapharma).
  2. Gather your documents: photo ID, proof of Social Security number and recent proof of address.
  3. Prepare your body. CSL advises you to “stay hydrated, eat a well-balanced, low-fat meal, and get a good night's sleep” (CSL).
  4. Block out 2 to 2.5 hours for the first visit, plus travel.
  5. Book your second visit inside the return window (2 to 14 days at Octapharma).
  6. Activate your card as soon as you receive it.
  7. Avoid ATM fees by using card purchases, or another method your card's fee schedule shows as free.
  8. Log every payment with the date and amount for tax time.
  9. Recalculate after the promotion ends. Use the regular per-visit fee to decide whether it is still worth your time. For other fast-paying options, see our extra money guide.

FAQ

Can I donate at two different companies to earn more?

Don't plan on it. The federal rule limits automated collection to no more than twice in a 7-day period, at least 2 days apart (21 CFR 640.65). The centers describe that limit as applying to you as a donor. Octapharma's FAQ has a section titled “What if I donated at another plasma center?” Read it, and be upfront with staff about any recent donations.

Why is my pay lower than the online calculator showed?

Octapharma says its calculator “is an estimate only, and does not include new or unique donor fees,” and that it “is programmed for returning plasma donors only” (FAQ). Your weight, height, hematocrit, location and current promotions all change the real figure. Third-party pay charts often don't show where their numbers come from.

How fast do I get paid?

Octapharma pays “within 24 hours on a prepaid debit card” (New Donors). BioLife says payment “typically happens shortly after your donation is complete” (BioLife). CSL pays on a prepaid card “after your first donation” (CSL).

Why did my plasma donation pay go down?

Most often, a new-donor promotion has ended. Octapharma says pay is “returning to the normal payment schedule when the promotional period has expired” (FAQ). At BioLife, weekly offers also expire at 11:59 p.m. each Sunday.

Do I get a tax form from the plasma center?

BioLife says donors “won't receive any tax documents” from it (BioLife). Keep your own records and ask a tax professional how to report the payments.
