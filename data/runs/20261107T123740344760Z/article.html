If you are asking what jobs make the most money, the US federal data gives a clear answer. The 20 occupations with the highest median annual wage are all in healthcare, led by surgeons and other medical specialists. For comparison, the median annual wage for all US occupations was $50,980 in May 2025, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS). Keep one limit in mind from the start: these figures are US data for wage and salary employees. Freelancers and business owners are not counted.

Every entry is a physician, a surgeon, a dental specialty or a nurse anesthetist. Specialty makes a large difference within medicine. General pediatricians had a median of $210,040 in May 2025, according to the BLS physicians and surgeons profile. Pediatric surgeons, at the top of the BLS list, had a median of $559,030. Some older articles quote earlier figures, so use the current OOH numbers when you compare jobs.

High earners outside the top-20 list

Several well-known careers don't appear on the top-20 list but still pay far above the all-occupation median. These are useful comparisons because their training paths look very different from medicine.

  • Airline pilots, copilots and flight engineers: $232,140 median in May 2025, according to the BLS pilots profile. That is just below the 20th entry on the BLS list, nurse anesthetists at $236,590. Commercial pilots are a separate BLS occupation (for example, charter or aerial work rather than scheduled airlines), and their median was $123,220.
  • Lawyers: $159,670 median, per the BLS lawyers profile.
  • Software developers: $135,980 median, per the BLS software developers profile.
  • Elevator and escalator installers and repairers: $109,910 median, per the BLS elevator installers profile. This one typically requires a high school diploma plus an apprenticeship rather than a college degree.

Physician or surgeon

According to the BLS, physicians and surgeons typically need a bachelor's degree and then a medical school degree, which takes another 4 years. Depending on the specialty, they also need 3 to 9 years in internship and residency programs, and subspecializing adds a fellowship of 1 to 3 years. All states require a license, and requirements vary by state. To qualify, candidates must graduate from an accredited medical school and complete residency training in their specialty. BLS notes that board certification is not required but may increase employment opportunities. The median for all physicians and surgeons was $275,930, with the lowest 10% earning less than $76,560 and the highest 10% more than $488,320.

Nurse anesthetist

Nurse anesthetists are advanced practice registered nurses (APRNs). The BLS says an APRN must have a registered nursing (RN) license before starting education in an advanced practice role. State requirements vary, but in general APRNs must have an RN license, complete an accredited graduate-level program, pass a national certification exam and hold an APRN license.

According to the AANA (the professional association for nurse anesthetists, not a regulator), applicants need a bachelor's or graduate degree in nursing or another appropriate major, an unencumbered RN and/or APRN license, and at least 1 year of full-time critical care experience as an RN, or the part-time equivalent. The AANA states that as of March 2026, "All nurse anesthesia programs approved to award doctoral degrees for entry into practice." It also says programs last 36 to 51 months, depending on the university.

Airline pilot

The BLS says airline pilots typically need a bachelor's degree and experience as a commercial or military pilot, and must meet Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) requirements. BLS lists the certificates in this order: student pilot certificate, private pilot certificate, instrument rating, commercial pilot certificate, multi-engine rating, and airline transport pilot (ATP) certificate. All pilot crews of a scheduled commercial airliner must have ATP certificates.

The flight-time requirement is set in federal regulation. Under 14 CFR 61.159, an applicant for an ATP certificate with an airplane category and class rating must have at least 1,500 hours of total time as a pilot, including at least 500 hours of cross-country time, 100 hours of night flight time and 50 hours in the class of airplane for the rating sought. The rule applies "except as provided in paragraphs (b), (c), and (d)," so read the current text if you think an exception might apply to you.

Lawyer

According to the BLS, becoming a lawyer usually takes 7 years of full-time study after high school: 4 years of undergraduate study and 3 years of law school. Lawyers typically need a law degree and a state license, which usually requires passing a bar exam. Each state's highest court sets its own rules for bar admission, so requirements depend on where you plan to practice. The median was $159,670, with the lowest 10% earning less than $78,360.

Software developer

The BLS says software developers typically need a bachelor's degree in computer and information technology or a related field, and some employers prefer candidates with a master's degree. Its median of $135,980 has a narrower spread than law or medicine: the lowest 10% earned less than $82,460 and the highest 10% more than $214,670. BLS also notes that within every occupation, earnings vary by experience, responsibility, performance, tenure and geographic area.

Elevator and escalator installer

This is the clearest high-paying example on this list that doesn't start with a college degree. The BLS says a high school diploma or equivalent is typically required, and the career usually begins with a 4-year apprenticeship sponsored by a union, industry association or employer. Each year, apprentices complete a set number of hours of technical instruction and paid on-the-job training. Most states require a license. It is a small occupation: BLS counted 24,700 jobs in 2025 and projects about 1,900 openings a year, so competition for apprenticeship spots can be a real factor.

To look for openings, the US Department of Labor's Apprenticeship Finder lets you search open apprenticeship opportunities from employers and apply directly with the employer running the program.

What the salary rankings don't tell you

Lists of the highest-paying jobs are a useful starting point, but the underlying data has limits that matter when you're making a decision.

  • A median is not starting pay. The BLS FAQ describes the median as a boundary between the highest-paid 50% and the lowest-paid 50% of workers. It is not a typical first-year figure. For physicians and surgeons, the lowest 10% earned less than $76,560, far below the $275,930 median.
  • The hours behind the pay. BLS says many physicians and surgeons work long shifts, which may include irregular and overnight hours or being on call. Airline pilots fly an average of 75 hours a month and spend about 150 more hours a month on other duties, such as checking weather and preparing flight plans. Their flight assignments and advancement are based on seniority. Some lawyers work more than 40 hours a week.
  • What counts as wages. According to the May 2025 OEWS technical notes, the wage figures are straight-time, gross pay. They include base rate, cost-of-living allowances, hazardous-duty pay, incentive pay (including commissions and production bonuses) and tips. They exclude overtime pay, severance pay, shift differentials, nonproduction bonuses, employer cost for benefits and tuition reimbursements. The BLS FAQ confirms the estimates do not include benefits. The technical notes also explain that an occupation's annual rate is calculated by multiplying the hourly wage rate by a typical work year of 2,080 hours.
  • "May 2025" covers three years of surveys. The BLS FAQ says the May 2025 estimates were based on survey panels from May 2025, November 2024, May 2024, November 2023, May 2023 and November 2022. The technical notes describe the full six-panel sample as 1.1 million establishments.
  • Self-employed people are not counted. The OEWS survey does not include the self-employed, owners and partners in unincorporated firms, household workers or unpaid family workers. The OOH Pay tab states the same for self-employed workers. If you freelance, run a sole proprietorship or own a share of an unincorporated business, these rankings say nothing about what you can earn.
  • It is US-only data. These are figures for jobs in the United States. If you live elsewhere, check your own country's national statistics office for comparable pay data.

How to check the pay for any job you're considering

You can run the same check for any occupation in a few minutes using the free BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook. The page layout may change, so tab names could differ slightly from what is described here.

  1. Open the job's profile in the OOH. Start from a page such as the Highest Paying Occupations list and click through to the occupation, or search for it by name.
  2. Read the Quick Facts table. It shows the median pay, the typical entry-level education and any on-the-job training.
  3. Open the Pay tab. Note the lowest-10% and highest-10% figures, not just the median, and look at pay by industry. Also check the note on who is excluded, such as self-employed workers.
  4. Open the State & Area Data tab. Pay varies by geographic area, so look at the figures for where you live or plan to work.
  5. Check licensing on the How to Become One tab. Many profiles link to CareerOneStop's license and certification finders, where you can see what your state requires.

Where freelancing and online work fit

Because the BLS rankings exclude self-employed income, they can't tell you how freelance, creator or small-business income compares with these jobs. Online income has to be judged differently: by what platforms take, how steady the work is and how much of your time it needs. FAQ

Which job pays the most?

On the BLS list of the 20 occupations with the highest median annual pay, pediatric surgeons rank first, with a May 2025 median of $559,030, according to the BLS Highest Paying Occupations page. Cardiologists ($496,010) and radiologists ($420,860) follow.

Can I get a high-paying job without a degree?

Some well-paid jobs don't start with a college degree. Elevator and escalator installers and repairers had a May 2025 median of $109,910, and the BLS says the job typically requires a high school diploma, followed by a 4-year apprenticeship with paid on-the-job training. Most states also require a license, and the occupation is small, so openings are limited.

Do these pay figures include bonuses and benefits?

Partly. The OEWS technical notes say commissions and production bonuses are included, but overtime, nonproduction bonuses and employer benefit costs are not. The BLS FAQ confirms benefits are excluded.

Are freelancers and business owners included in these rankings?

No. The OEWS survey behind the OOH pay figures does not include the self-employed or owners and partners in unincorporated firms, so these rankings cover wage and salary employees only.
