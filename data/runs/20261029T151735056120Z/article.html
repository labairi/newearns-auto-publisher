There is no single best time to post on Instagram that works for every account. Four large studies, from Hootsuite, Sprout Social, Buffer and Later, each give a starting point, but they reach different answers. One names 5 AM as the top slot, while two others warn against early mornings. This guide puts the studies side by side, explains why they disagree, and then walks through a free four-week test with Instagram's own Insights so you can find the times that work for your followers.

Where they agree

  • Midweek is strong. Sprout names Monday to Thursday as the best days, Buffer names Wednesday as the best day overall, and Hootsuite's best days are Monday, Tuesday and Thursday.
  • Saturday is weak. Sprout lists weekends as the worst days, Buffer lists Friday and Saturday as the weakest, and Later calls Saturday its worst day overall.
  • Friday is unremarkable. Sprout found no significant peak engagement hours on Fridays, and Buffer ranks Friday among its weakest days.

Where they disagree

  • Early morning. Later found posts published between 3 AM and 6 AM led to higher-than-average engagement every day. Sprout calls 3–7 a.m. on weekdays an "early morning dead zone", and Hootsuite says the least effective times are typically before 8 AM.
  • Evening vs afternoon. Buffer found 6–11 p.m. the strongest window on every weekday except Thursday. Sprout's top windows are mostly early to mid afternoon, with Wednesday stretching to 9 p.m. Hootsuite's slots cluster in the late afternoon, and it flags after 10 PM as weak.

Why they differ

The studies measure different things in different ways:

  • Different accounts. Each study draws on its own customers' posts, so the results reflect the kinds of accounts that use that tool. Sprout's and Hootsuite's samples also span several social networks, not just Instagram.
  • Different periods. Later's data ends in October 2024; Sprout's covers late 2025 to early 2026.
  • Different metrics. Buffer uses median engagement rate, Later uses engagement rate, and Sprout reports engagement windows. A post can rank well on one measure and poorly on another.
  • Time zones. Hootsuite localised times across 118 countries, Sprout lists all times in local time, and Buffer says "12 p.m. Wednesday means 12 p.m. local time wherever you are." Later says it analysed posts across time zones.

The practical takeaway: treat any study's slot as a hypothesis to test, not an answer.

Why timing matters at all

Instagram's own explanation of ranking gives timing a role, but not the leading one. In "Instagram Ranking Explained" (Adam Mosseri, May 31, 2023), Instagram says each part of the app, including Feed, Stories, Explore and Reels, uses its own algorithm. For Feed, it lists signals "roughly in order of importance": first comes the viewer's own activity, then information about the post, which includes "how quickly people are liking, commenting, sharing and saving a post" and when it was posted.

So posting when your audience is around can help a post pick up early interactions, but the viewer's activity is listed ahead of it. Two caveats: the post is from 2023, and Instagram says it adds and removes signals and predictions over time. Good content posted at an average time will usually matter more than a mediocre post at a perfect time.

How to see when your followers are online

What you need

Follower activity data lives in Insights, which requires a professional account. Instagram's creator site says to switch to a professional account to access insights, and Buffer notes you need a Creator or Business account to see these numbers. Switching is done in your account settings.

Steps in the app (labels vary)

Instagram's Help Center steps could not be confirmed for this guide, and the vendor guides describe the path with different labels, so menu names in your app may differ. The general route is:

  1. Open your profile.
  2. Tap Professional dashboard or Insights.
  3. Open the followers section. Depending on your app version, this may be labelled New followers, Total followers or similar.
  4. Scroll to the active-times chart, labelled something like Most active times or Follower active times. It typically shows activity by hour and by day.

For reference, Sprout describes the path as Insights → New Followers → Most Active Times, Buffer as Professional dashboard → New followers → Follower active times, and Later as Total Followers → Most Active Times. Buffer also describes a web route: Dashboard → Insights → Account → scroll to Followers.

Active isn't the same as engaged

As Buffer puts it, these are times your followers are using Instagram, "not necessarily" when they are most likely to engage with your content. A peak at 9 PM might be people scrolling passively before bed. That is why the chart is a starting point for testing rather than a final answer.

A 4-week test to find your own best time

This is an editorial method, not a published study. It uses only native Insights and a spreadsheet.

  1. Pick two or three candidate windows. For example: Slot A is the peak from your active-times chart; Slot B is a study benchmark such as Wednesday 12 p.m. from Buffer or Tuesday afternoon from Sprout; Slot C is a wildcard, such as an early-morning slot to check Later's finding for your audience.
  2. Keep everything else steady. Use the same format and a similar content type in each slot. Comparing a Reel at 9 AM against a carousel at 6 PM tells you about format, not timing.
  3. Rotate the slots across days. If Slot A always lands on Monday and Slot C always on Friday, you are partly testing days. Shift the order each week so every slot gets a turn on different days.
  4. Record the same numbers for every post: date, day, time, format, reach, shares, saves and comments. Check each post at the same age, for example three days after publishing, so older posts don't get a head start.
  5. Compare after about four weeks. Average each slot's results. If one slot clearly leads on reach and shares, make it your default and keep the runner-up as a second option. If the numbers are close, treat it as a tie: a few posts per slot is a small sample.
  6. Re-check later. Hootsuite suggests reviewing your posting schedule once or twice a year, or whenever something major changes, such as a new audience, a new content format or a big jump in followers.

If logging posts by hand gets tedious, tools such as Instagramaps compare views, reach and engagement per post, which makes it easier to line up your slots side by side.

Followers in several time zones

First find out where your followers actually are; our guide to Instagram follower location covers how to see which countries and cities they're in. Then Sprout advises identifying your highest-value regions and anchoring your schedule to their local peak hours. Hootsuite suggests planning posts that straddle time zones, or posting once in the morning for a North American audience and again in the late evening for an overseas audience. Tools such as Instagramaps also let creators and businesses connect an Instagram account to see where their audience engages by country and city, which helps you decide which region to anchor to.

Reels

The studies split here. Sprout says the best times for Reels mirror its overall windows (Monday 2–4 p.m. through Thursday 12–2 p.m.). Buffer reviewed all post types together rather than by format. Later's analysis of over 975K Reels points to 12 AM, with Monday the best day. If Reels are your main format, run your test with Reels only.

Local businesses

If your customers are mostly in one city, the time-zone question disappears and their daily routine matters more. Sprout and Hootsuite include industry breakdowns on their pages; use them to pick test slots, then let your own results decide.

Common mistakes

  • Treating one study as gospel. As the table shows, credible studies with millions of posts still disagree about early mornings and evenings.
  • Chasing the exact minute. Later says consistency matters more than posting at the exact same time. A predictable rhythm is easier to keep up than a precise slot.
  • Reading active times as engagement times. The Insights chart shows when followers are on Instagram, not when they interact with you.
  • Changing several things at once. New time, new format and new caption style in the same week make it impossible to tell what worked.
  • Never re-checking. Audiences change. Put a review in your calendar.

FAQ

What is the best day to post on Instagram?

It depends on the study. Buffer names Wednesday, Sprout names Monday to Thursday, and Hootsuite names Monday, Tuesday and Thursday, while noting the best day depends on your audience, niche and time zone.

What is the worst time to post on Instagram?

Hootsuite points to before 8 AM and after 10 PM, and Sprout flags 3–7 a.m. on weekdays. Later disagrees on early mornings and names Thursday 3 AM and Saturday 11 PM as its worst slots. Saturday ranks low in Sprout, Buffer and Later.

Does my time zone matter?

Yes, in the sense that the studies report times in local time, so "12 p.m." means midday where your audience is. If followers are spread out, anchor to your highest-value region's local peak.

Should I post at the same time every day?

Not necessarily. Later says consistency matters more than posting at the exact same time. Pick a few tested windows and stick to a rhythm you can maintain.
