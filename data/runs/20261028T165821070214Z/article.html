Real remote jobs with no experience do exist, but they make up a small slice of what's posted. In a Robert Half analysis of US job postings, only 2% of entry-level postings in the professional fields it covers were fully remote. Your best odds are roles that employers train beginners for, found through filtered or official job sources and confirmed on the company's own website before you apply. One rule never bends: according to the FTC, honest employers will never ask you to pay to get a job.

This guide covers job searches in the United States and official US sites.

How many entry-level jobs are actually fully remote

Staffing firm Robert Half analyzed US job postings from the data provider TalentNeuron. The postings were mapped to more than 450 job titles in finance and accounting, technology, marketing and creative, legal, administrative and customer support, human resources and healthcare support. That is the scope behind every figure in this section: postings in the fields Robert Half covers, not the whole US job market (Robert Half, Remote work statistics and trends).

Within that scope, Robert Half reports that in Q2 2026, 87% of analyzed postings were fully on-site, 10% were hybrid and 3% were fully remote. The fully in-office share rose from 65% in Q4 2025 to 87% in Q2 2026 (Robert Half).

The split by years of relevant experience, again for postings in the fields Robert Half covers (source):

Experience levelHybrid postingsFully remote postings

The field most beginners look at first is no better. For administrative and customer support postings in Robert Half's analysis, 93% were fully on-site, 5% hybrid and 2% fully remote (Robert Half).

What these numbers mean for your search

Robert Half says the shift means more competition for fully remote roles (source). If you apply only to fully remote listings and hear nothing back, that does not necessarily mean you are doing something wrong. Three practical adjustments follow from the data:

  • Apply widely. When few postings match, a short list of applications will rarely be enough.
  • Treat hybrid as a possible first step. In the analysis above, entry-level hybrid postings outnumbered fully remote ones four to one (8% vs. 2%).
  • Target roles where employers do the training. That way "no experience" is expected, not a disadvantage.

Remote-friendly roles employers train beginners for

Customer service representative

This is one of the clearest examples of an occupation that hires people without prior experience and trains them on the job. According to the Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook:

  • Education: The typical entry-level education is a high school diploma or equivalent, though BLS notes some workers have postsecondary education, including bachelor's degrees.
  • Training: Short-term on-the-job training, which typically lasts 2 to 4 weeks. Those in finance and insurance may need several months of training to learn complicated financial regulations.
  • Pay: The 2025 median pay was $44,770 per year, or $21.53 per hour. In May 2025, the lowest 10% earned less than $15.27 an hour and the highest 10% earned more than $30.57.
  • Remote possibility: BLS says working from home is possible in some companies, not all.
  • Outlook: Employment is projected to decline 5% from 2025 to 2035, but about 289,500 openings are still projected each year, on average. All of them are expected to come from replacing workers who move to other occupations or leave the labor force.

These figures describe the occupation as a whole, not remote positions specifically. The job also has real drawbacks. BLS notes that representatives may be under pressure to answer a set number of calls while supervisors monitor them for quality assurance, and the work can be stressful when dealing with dissatisfied customers. Representatives who give information about finance and insurance may need a state license, which usually includes passing an exam (BLS).

Other job titles to search for

These search terms can surface similar beginner-friendly listings:

  • "customer support"
  • "chat support"
  • "administrative assistant"
  • "virtual assistant"

Pay, hours and requirements vary by employer, so take them from the specific posting, not from a generic list. For each listing, look for the job location line (does it say fully remote, hybrid or a city?), any time-zone or hours requirement, the training period and how you apply.

A warning that applies to several of these titles: the FTC has found hijacked job ads offering work from home as a personal assistant or customer service representative, which then ask for Social Security and bank account numbers (FTC). Its job scams guide also describes virtual assistant offers built around depositing a check (FTC). Where to search and how to filter for fully remote jobs

The FTC notes that scammers advertise jobs the same way honest employers do, in online ads, on job sites and on social media (FTC). Where you search matters, but checking each listing matters more.

Company careers pages

The FTC's advice is to verify job openings before you apply by visiting the official website of the organization or company. It notes most include a "career opportunities" or "jobs" section (FTC). Use this as your final check no matter where you first saw the job.

USAJOBS (federal jobs)

The FTC calls USAJobs.gov the federal government's official job site (FTC). USAJOBS defines a remote job as one where you are not expected to report regularly to an official agency worksite and instead work from home or another alternative worksite (USAJOBS Help Center).

USAJOBS describes two ways to find remote jobs. USAJOBS may change its layout; these steps follow its help page.

  1. Location search: Enter "remote" in the Location search box at the top of any page. You may need to click the Search icon at the top of the page to display the Location box. Select from the auto-populated choices, then click Search.
  2. Remote jobs filter: Scroll down to Remote jobs, open the options and select "Only show remote jobs."

What to check in each announcement, according to USAJOBS:

  • The label. A remote job is listed as "Anywhere in the U.S. (remote job)" under the Location header in the Overview section.
  • Telework is not remote. USAJOBS states that a telework-eligible job is not the same as a remote position. In practice, read "telework" as hybrid.
  • Occasional travel. Remote workers may occasionally travel to an office for training or other reasons, but do not commute there for day-to-day work.
  • Time zones. Some government jobs may require you to work hours in a different time zone from the one you live in.
  • Locality pay. For a remote job, the locality adjustment is based on where you regularly work, meaning your home or alternative worksite.

Read each announcement's qualifications carefully, since the filter shows remote jobs at every level, not only entry-level ones.

CareerOneStop (US Department of Labor)

CareerOneStop is sponsored by the US Department of Labor, and the FTC lists it as a source for finding jobs (FTC). Its help page points to a "Find a Remote Job" search for jobs that don't require you to commute or be onsite every day. Some of those listings are entirely work-from-home, and some may require onsite time (CareerOneStop Job Finder Help).

Useful details from the same help page (layouts may differ from what's described):

  • Date filter: You can filter by date posted. Choosing "1 Day Back" shows only jobs posted in the past day.
  • Listing quality: Some postings come from commercial job banks that don't share the same posting requirements, so some may be duplicates and some may not be for real, existing openings.
  • Applying: Clicking "Apply" takes you to the hiring company's website.
  • Who posts: The page says any employer may post jobs there, so still verify each employer.

Big job boards (Indeed, LinkedIn)

Large job boards have remote or location filters, but menus and labels vary and change, so use whatever remote or location option the site currently shows. The key point is verification: the FTC reports that scammers take outdated ads from real employers, alter them and post them on platforms like Indeed or LinkedIn. Some then claim you got the job and send a check to buy equipment (FTC). Find every promising listing on the company's own careers page before you apply.

A five-step plan for this week

This plan won't produce income on a schedule, but it focuses your effort where the odds are better and the risks are lower.

  1. Pick one or two target roles. Customer service is a sensible start, since BLS says representatives typically receive on-the-job training that usually lasts 2 to 4 weeks (BLS).
  2. Run the official searches. Use the USAJOBS remote filter (steps) and CareerOneStop's remote search, with the date filter set to recent postings (CareerOneStop). Save hybrid listings too.
  3. Verify on the company's own site. Before you apply, find the same opening in the employer's careers or jobs section, as the FTC advises.
  4. Tailor your resume to communication. BLS lists communication skills as an important quality for customer service representatives: providing clear information in writing, by phone or in person (BLS). Point to concrete examples from school, volunteering or past jobs where you did exactly that.
  5. Search before you accept. Look up the company or recruiter name plus "scam," "review" or "complaint." The FTC notes that finding no complaints doesn't guarantee a company is honest (FTC).

If you already paid or shared information

The FTC advises contacting the company you used to send the money right away, reporting the fraud and asking to have the transaction reversed, if possible. Report the scam to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and you can also report it to your state attorney general (FTC).

FAQ

Do I need a degree for remote jobs with no experience?

It depends on the role. For customer service representatives, BLS says the typical entry-level education is a high school diploma or equivalent (BLS). Check each posting for its own requirements.

Is a hybrid job worth taking?

It can be a realistic first step. In Robert Half's analysis of postings in the fields it covers, 8% of entry-level postings were hybrid and 2% fully remote (Robert Half). On USAJOBS, remember that "telework-eligible" does not mean remote (USAJOBS).

Should I ever pay for training or equipment to get hired?

No. The FTC says honest employers, including the federal government, will never ask you to pay to get a job, and anyone who does is a scammer (FTC).

Can a job offer by text be real?

The FTC advises ignoring generic and unexpected texts, WhatsApp or Telegram messages about jobs, and says real employers will never contact you that way (FTC).

The realistic route

Fully remote entry-level jobs are scarce, so focus on roles employers train for, search official and filtered sources, keep hybrid roles on your list and confirm every opening on the employer's own site.
