The TikTok Creator Rewards Program is how TikTok pays creators directly for eligible videos that run at least one minute. It replaced the Creativity Program Beta, and TikTok does not publish a fixed pay rate. This guide sticks to what TikTok's own US and EEA terms actually say, separates the two regions where their rules differ, and shows how to work out your real rate from your own numbers.

A note on sources: this guide is built from TikTok's published legal terms and its launch announcement. TikTok's in-app screens and regional help pages may show extra detail for your country, so treat the app as the final word on whether you can join.

What the Creator Rewards Program is

TikTok announced on March 18, 2024 that the Creativity Program was out of beta and relaunched as the Creator Rewards Program, according to the TikTok Newsroom launch post. The US Creator Rewards Program Terms still describe it as the program "fka the Creativity Program Beta".

At launch in March 2024, TikTok said the rewards formula focused on four core metrics: originality, play duration (watch time and finish rate), search value and audience engagement. It also said creators would be rewarded for their account's "ad value", based on their community's ad watch time (TikTok Newsroom). That was the launch description, not a published current formula.

The current terms describe rewards more generally, and the two regions word it differently:

  • US: rewards are based on "total legitimate video views and other signals reflecting the popularity of, and engagement with, Eligible Videos", in TikTok's sole discretion (US terms, §2).
  • EEA: "Rewards are calculated dynamically based on the country of the video views origin, the number of video views, video engagement and authenticity of a video view" (EEA terms).

Who can join: the requirements

Account requirements

The checklist below comes from the US terms (§1) unless marked EEA. TikTok says these requirements "may be updated at any time in our sole discretion".

  • Age: 18 or older, or the age of majority where you live.
  • Residence: a legal resident of a country where the program is available. The EEA terms add that you must "not use a VPN or other similar tool".
  • Followers: at least 10,000 authentic followers.
  • Views: at least 100,000 authentic total video views in the 30 days before you apply (or are invited).
  • Standing: an account in good standing (stated in the EEA terms and in TikTok's 2024 launch post).
  • Payment and tax: a valid linked payment account "registered in Creator's own name", plus completed onboarding documentation, including any tax reporting documents.
  • Account type: not a Business Account or Organization Account, and not an account belonging to a government organization, politician or political party. The EEA terms also say your account must not be a Business Account.

Where it's available

TikTok's EEA terms say only that the program "is available only in selected countries, whether you are entitled to participate will depend on your country of residence" (EEA terms). The terms read for this guide don't list the countries, so check eligibility inside the TikTok app rather than relying on country lists from third-party sites.

Which videos earn rewards

Both regions require videos of at least one minute. The EEA terms spell out a fuller list. Under those terms, a video must meet all of these criteria:

  • one minute or longer
  • at least 1,000 qualified views
  • not a Duet or Stitch video
  • not a Photo Mode video
  • not an advertisement, paid promotion or sponsored content
  • original content produced entirely by the creator, and/or adds new ideas to preexisting content
  • "high quality as determined by TikTok in its sole discretion"
  • complies with the terms, Community Guidelines and Copyright Policy

The EEA terms also say plainly that "not all content you post to participate in the Creator Rewards Program will collect Rewards."

What counts as a qualified view (EEA)

In the EEA terms, a qualified view is one where the viewer watched for five seconds or more and did not mark the video as "not interested". Repeat views from the same account count as one view. Your own views and artificially produced views are excluded (EEA terms).

What the US terms say

The US terms are shorter on video rules. They require eligible content "with a duration of at least 1 minute publicly posted on the Platform" and exclude metrics that are artificially produced or obtained, paid or incentivized, or generated by you or accounts acting with you to inflate numbers. The 1,000-qualified-views rule and the Duet, Stitch and Photo Mode exclusions appear in the EEA terms, not in the US terms read for this guide. The US terms also let TikTok "mute, remove or disqualify your Eligible Videos if they contain unauthorized uses of music" (§4).

How much the Creator Rewards Program pays

TikTok publishes no fixed rate. In the US, rewards are decided "in its sole discretion", and TikTok "reserves the right to impose a monthly or annual cap on each Creator's available rewards" (US terms, §2). The US terms also mention a "monthly Specialized Rewards Program" that creators may join by invitation.

The most widely quoted figure is a third-party estimate. Influencer Marketing Hub estimates that TikTok pays between $0.40 and $1.00 per 1,000 views through the Creator Rewards Program, and says earnings depend "heavily on engagement, watch time, and audience location." That is the publication's estimate; it doesn't explain how it was calculated, and TikTok hasn't confirmed it.

Work out your own rate

Your own dashboard is a better guide than any public figure. TikTok's 2024 launch post said the dashboard gives each video dedicated pages for RPM and qualified video views (TikTok Newsroom). To calculate your rate per 1,000 views:

Estimated rewards ÷ qualified views × 1,000 = your rate per 1,000 views

A hypothetical example: if a video showed $30 in estimated rewards on 60,000 qualified views, that's $30 ÷ 60,000 × 1,000 = $0.50 per 1,000 views. Track this across several videos and months; a single video tells you little.

To see what Influencer Marketing Hub's estimated range would mean in practice, here is the arithmetic only, not a forecast:

  • 100,000 views × $0.40 ÷ 1,000 = $40
  • 100,000 views × $1.00 ÷ 1,000 = $100

Note that $40 is below the $50 minimum payment threshold in TikTok's terms (covered below). At $0.40 per 1,000 you would need 125,000 views to reach $50; at $1.00, 50,000 views. Your actual rate may fall outside that range, and TikTok may cap rewards.

Why public pay figures vary so much

  • Confidentiality (US): the US terms (§6) say the details of reporting metrics TikTok provides "shall be kept strictly confidential by Creator", with an exception for legal or professional advisors bound by confidentiality. That helps explain why public pay figures tend to be informal. If you're in the US, think twice before posting screenshots of your dashboard.
  • Viewer location and engagement: the EEA terms base rewards partly on the country the views come from and on engagement, so two videos with the same view count can pay differently.
  • Discretion: TikTok decides rewards in its sole discretion and may cap them (US).

How and when you get paid

Both the US terms (§3) and the EEA terms describe the same basic schedule:

  • Payments are processed by PayPal through Hyperwallet.
  • The dashboard shows estimated rewards in US dollars and updates daily.
  • Rewards are calculated in the month after they're earned.
  • They're paid on the 15th of the month if the payable amount meets the $50.00 USD minimum (EEA: "or its equivalent in local currency").
  • When the money actually arrives depends on the payment provider (US terms).
  • Fees may apply. In the US, the creator "agrees to bear the cost" of transfer fees; in the EEA, your payment provider "might charge you fees".

The US terms also say TikTok "reserves the right to change the Minimum Payment Threshold."

Taxes

Under the US terms (§2), the creator "has sole responsibility and liability for any and all taxes", and TikTok may withhold amounts it is required to withhold. The US Rewards Policy says the same: you're responsible for taxes on rewards, and TikTok may withhold or deduct tax. In the EEA, providing tax information is part of onboarding and is required to receive rewards.

For US readers, the IRS's gig-economy guidance says income from part-time, temporary or side work must be reported on a tax return even if it isn't reported on a form such as a 1099-K, 1099-MISC or 1099-NEC. Keep your own record of payouts rather than waiting for a form.

The fine print most guides skip

  1. Meeting the requirements doesn't mean you get in or get paid. The US Rewards Policy says meeting eligibility requirements "does not guarantee your participation", and TikTok keeps "full discretion on whether to provide Rewards and the nature and amount of Rewards." The EEA terms add that payments "do not reflect or guarantee your entitlement to any future payment."
  2. Rejected? Wait 30 days (US). "If your application is rejected, you may re-apply after 30 days" (US terms).
  3. Possible reward cap (US). TikTok may impose a monthly or annual cap on each creator's rewards.
  4. Clawbacks (US). TikTok may offset future rewards by any amount paid in error. If no future payments are due, you must refund the amount within 30 days of written notice (US §3).
  5. Confidential metrics (US). Reporting metrics and other non-public program information must be kept strictly confidential (US §6).
  6. Disclosure when you promote TikTok (US). You must "clearly and conspicuously disclose" your participation when endorsing TikTok, for example with #Ad (US §6).
  7. Licence to your name and content. The US terms grant TikTok a royalty-free licence to use your user name, image, voice, likeness and content for marketing and promoting TikTok to advertisers. The EEA terms say TikTok may show your content to advertisers and brands.
  8. Suspension for public statements or situations. The EEA terms allow suspension or termination if you make "any derogatory public statement or comment concerning TikTok" or are involved in a situation "which reflects unfavourably upon you or TikTok". The US terms cover situations "that could reflect unfavorably upon Creator or TikTok". The US terms say you may appeal a termination "within the prescribed timeframe".
  9. You're a contractor (US). The relationship is "that of independent contractors", and the creator is "not an employee of TikTok" (US §7).
  10. Terms and rewards can change. TikTok may add or modify eligibility requirements in its sole discretion, and "may modify or cease rewards" with "reasonable advance notice" (US §2). The Rewards Policy says eligibility may change "at any time and without prior notice" unless the law requires otherwise.

In the EEA, you can leave the program by contacting TikTok through the Feedback functionality, according to the EEA terms.

What to do this week

Whether or not you qualify yet, these steps put you in a position to apply and to judge the program on real numbers. If you're building income from mobile tools more broadly, see how to earn money from your phone.

  1. Check your age and account type. Business and Organization accounts aren't eligible, so if you use a Business account, you would need a personal account.
  2. Check your numbers. Compare your followers against 10,000 and your total views over the last 30 days against 100,000. TikTok's terms say both must be authentic.
  3. Get payment and tax details ready. Set up a PayPal account in your own name and have your tax information ready, since onboarding requires both.
  4. Plan original videos of a minute or more. Audit your recent content: under the EEA terms, Duets, Stitches, Photo Mode posts and sponsored videos don't qualify, and both regions exclude artificial or paid views. Check that any music you use is authorised.
  5. Apply in the app. The US terms say you "submit a complete Program application via the Platform" or are invited by TikTok. In 2024, TikTok said its monetisation tools sit in the in-app Creator tools (TikTok Newsroom). Menu names may differ by app version and region.
  6. Start a simple log. Once you're in, record each video's estimated rewards and qualified views monthly so you can calculate your own rate per 1,000 views.

Is it worth it?

For creators who already meet the thresholds and post original videos over a minute, the program is a way to earn from views without negotiating anything. But the terms leave the rate, caps and final decision to TikTok, and in the US balances under $50 may expire. Influencer Marketing Hub describes view payouts as a small piece of most creators' TikTok income compared with brand deals, affiliate sales and TikTok Shop.

A sensible approach is to treat Creator Rewards as one income stream, measure your actual rate after a few months, and compare it with other options. For a wider view of platform income and what each platform takes, see How to Make Money Online: 5 Ways and What Each Platform Takes.

FAQ

Do Duets and Stitches count for Creator Rewards?

Under the EEA terms, no: eligible videos must not be a Duet or Stitch, or a Photo Mode video. The US terms read for this guide don't list these exclusions, so US creators should check the requirements shown in the app.

Is there a fee to join?

The EEA terms say "Participation in Creator Rewards Program doesn't require payment." Payment providers may charge fees on payouts, and in the US the creator bears transfer fees (US terms, §3).

When do I get paid?

Rewards are calculated in the month after they're earned and paid on the 15th if you've reached the $50 minimum. Arrival time depends on the payment provider (US terms).

What happens if I earn less than $50?

It depends on your region. In the US, unpaid amounts below the threshold "may be deemed expired" at TikTok's discretion. In the EEA, the amount "will be transferred to you as set out on the Balance dashboard" (EEA terms).

Can I reapply if I'm rejected?

Under the US terms, yes, after 30 days.
