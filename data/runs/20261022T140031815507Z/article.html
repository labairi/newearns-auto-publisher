Instagram can show you the top countries and cities your followers come from. Insights report these as totals for your audience, not a location for each follower. This guide covers the current steps for checking your Instagram follower location data in the app and on a computer, what your account needs before locations appear, and why the numbers don't always add up.

What Instagram shows about follower location (and what it doesn't)

Total locations, not individual followers

Instagram Insights summarise your audience as a group. Meta's developer documentation describes follower demographics as "the demographic characteristics of followers, including countries, cities and gender distribution" (Meta for Developers). That is a breakdown such as "a share of followers in one city", not a list of who lives where.

Insights are also private to you. According to Instapect's 2026 guide, they are visible only to the account owner and people the owner gives access to.

Followers vs viewers vs engaged accounts

"Location" can mean three different groups, and Instagram reports them separately. Knowing which one you're looking at stops a lot of confusion.

So your viewer locations may lean towards places that barely show up in your follower locations. If you want to know where your existing community lives, look at followers. If you want to know where your content is landing right now, look at viewers.

What you need before locations appear

  • A public account. Meta's current help page says: "You need a public account on Instagram to view insights" (Meta Help Center). Personal accounts use Your dashboard; professional accounts use Professional dashboard.
  • At least 100 followers for follower trends. Instagram's help pages say you need at least 100 followers to see follower trends such as growth, top locations, age range and most active times, according to Lilach Bullock and Zolyp. Meta's developer documentation applies the same rule to its API: follower demographics are "not returned if the IG User has less than 100 followers" (Meta for Developers).
  • A preset timeframe and more than 100 viewers for audience demographics. Meta's wording: "To view audience demographics, use a preset timeframe and reach or engage over 100 viewers" (Meta Help Center).
  • The past 90 days only, by default. Looking back beyond 90 days and exporting insights are only available to eligible public accounts with a Meta One Advanced, Expert or Max subscription, and not to teens (Meta Help Center).

Some guides say you need a Business or Creator account. Sprout Social's guide, for example, says business accounts with more than 100 followers can view trends including top locations and suggests switching to a professional account (Sprout Social). Lilach Bullock notes that an older Instagram help article still says insights are for businesses, while newer articles include public personal accounts (Lilach Bullock). This guide follows Meta's newer help page, which covers any public account.

How to see your followers' top locations in the Instagram app

Steps 1 to 4 and 6 follow Meta's help page on account insights; step 5 comes from Instapect's and Zolyp's guides. Menu labels change between app versions, so your screen may look slightly different.

  1. Tap Profile or your profile picture in the bottom right to go to your profile.
  2. If you have a professional account, tap Professional dashboard. If you have a personal account, tap Your dashboard.
  3. If you don't see a dashboard on your profile, tap More to open Settings and activity. Then tap Insights below For professionals (professional accounts) or Your dashboard below Your insights and tools (personal accounts).
  4. On the overview you'll see insights for Views, Interactions, New followers and Content you shared.
  5. Tap See all insights if your dashboard shows it, then tap Total followers (Instapect). Zolyp says this section shows top locations once you have at least 100 followers.
  6. To change the period, tap the dropdown menu, choose a preset timeframe or set a custom start and end date, then tap Update. Use a preset if you want demographics to load.

For a quick example: if you run a small baking business and see that most followers sit in your home city, with a second cluster in another country, that tells you both where local promotions will land and whether some posts should be scheduled for a second time zone.

How to check follower locations on a computer

The instagram.com dashboard

Zolyp's guide describes the desktop route like this. The layout may differ on your account.

  1. Go to instagram.com and log in.
  2. Open Dashboard.
  3. Choose Insights below For professionals, or Your dashboard below Your insights and tools for a personal account.
  4. Pick Account or Content.
  5. Set the date range. According to Zolyp, the options are 7, 14, 30 or 90 days, or a custom range.

Meta Business Suite

If your Instagram is a professional account connected to your business tools, Meta Business Suite offers another view. According to Instapect, Insights → Audience includes age, gender, top interests, locations, follower growth and active times, and you need permission for that Instagram profile. Zapier's guide also points to the Insights option in Business Suite's left menu, though it was written for an older version of the interface.

Why the location numbers may not add up

If the location figures cover fewer people than your follower count, nothing is broken. Meta's developer documentation for business and creator accounts lists several limits on demographic data (Meta for Developers):

  • Demographic metrics only return the top 45 performers, so smaller cities and countries drop off the list.
  • Only viewers Meta has demographic data for are counted.
  • Because of that, summing the values can give a number lower than your follower count.
  • The data used for metrics may be delayed by up to 48 hours.

Meta documents these limits for its API. In practice: expect a list of your biggest locations, not a complete map, and don't treat yesterday's new followers as missing if they haven't appeared yet. If demographics are blank, switch from a custom range to a preset, as Meta's help page requires (Meta Help Center).

Can you see where one specific follower is?

Not through Insights. They only show totals, and they only cover your own account, so you can't open insights for someone else's profile either. If you manage a client's account, ask them to give you access through their business tools; Instapect notes that insights are visible to the owner and people the owner gives access to (Instapect).

The other source of confusion is the Instagram Map. According to Meta's announcement on 6 August 2025:

  • Location sharing is off unless you opt in, and you can turn it off at any time.
  • You choose who sees your last active location: friends (followers you follow back), Close Friends, only selected friends, or no one.
  • You can choose not to share in specific places or with specific people.
  • The map sits at the top of your DM inbox. It rolled out in the US first, with "more global availability soon", so availability may vary where you are.
  • If parental supervision is set up, parents can control a teen's location sharing and are notified when the teen starts sharing.

In other words, the Map lets you opt in to sharing your own last active location with friends you choose (Meta).

Going further: mapping engagement by country and city

Native Insights are free and, for most freelancers and small businesses, enough. Follower locations are totals for your whole account, and for posts that reach more than 100 accounts, Insights also show top countries and cities of the accounts reached (Sprout Social). If you want a separate dashboard for this, Instagramaps lets creators and businesses connect an Instagram account to see where their audience engages by country and city. It also compares views, reach and engagement per post.

It also includes a marketplace where creators and businesses create profiles, get matched and send collaboration requests. Other analytics tools offer follower demographics too, so compare a few against what you actually need.

How to use follower location data

The data is only useful if it changes what you do. A few practical ideas:

  • Schedule for the right time zone. If your top locations sit in one region, post when that region is awake. Cross-check with most active times, which guides say also appears once you have at least 100 followers (Lilach Bullock).
  • Match offers to where people are. A local service business can promote in-person deals if followers are nearby; a digital product seller with a spread-out audience might focus on shipping-free or online offers instead.
  • Check language and references. If a large share of followers is in another country, consider captions, currencies or examples that make sense there too.
  • Compare followers with viewers. If viewers come from places your followers don't, some content is attracting a new audience. Note which posts did that.
  • Add it to client reports. If you manage social accounts for clients, a monthly snapshot of top cities and countries is easy to explain and helps set goals. If that's your business model, our guide on how to start a digital marketing agency covers the wider setup.

Because default history only covers the past 90 days (Meta Help Center), keep your own record if you want to track trends over a year.

FAQ

Do I need a business account to see follower locations?

According to Meta's current help page, you need a public account; both personal and professional accounts can view insights (Meta Help Center). Some guides still say you need a business or professional account (Sprout Social).

Why don't my location numbers add up to my follower count?

Meta's developer documentation says demographic metrics return only the top 45 performers and use only viewers Meta has demographic data for, so the values can sum to less than your follower count (Meta for Developers). Meta documents these limits for its API.

Can my followers see my location?

Not through Insights, which only the owner and people they give access to can see (Instapect). On the Instagram Map, location sharing is off unless you opt in and choose who to share with (Meta).

How far back can I check my Instagram follower location data?

The past 90 days by default. Looking back further and exporting insights require an eligible Meta One Advanced, Expert or Max plan (Meta Help Center).
